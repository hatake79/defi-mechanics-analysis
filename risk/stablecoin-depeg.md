# Stablecoin Depeg Risk

Stablecoins are designed to maintain a stable value, typically pegged to a fiat currency such as the US dollar.

However, stablecoins can lose their peg under certain market conditions.

This event is known as a **depeg**.

---

# Types of Stablecoins

Stablecoins use different mechanisms to maintain price stability.

## Fiat-Collateralized Stablecoins

These stablecoins are backed by off-chain reserves such as bank deposits or treasury assets.

Example structure:

1 token = 1 USD reserve.

---

## Crypto-Collateralized Stablecoins

These stablecoins are backed by crypto assets locked in smart contracts.

Because collateral assets are volatile, these systems typically require overcollateralization.

---

## Algorithmic Stablecoins

Algorithmic stablecoins attempt to maintain their peg through supply adjustments and economic incentives.

These designs are generally more fragile during market stress.

---

# Causes of Depeg Events

Stablecoins may lose their peg due to several factors.

## Liquidity Crisis

If large holders attempt to exit simultaneously, liquidity may become insufficient to maintain the peg.

---

## Collateral Failure

If the value of collateral assets drops rapidly, the stablecoin may become undercollateralized.

---

## Market Panic

Loss of confidence can trigger large sell-offs even if the underlying system remains solvent.

---

# Consequences of Depegging

A stablecoin losing its peg can affect many parts of the DeFi ecosystem.

Potential consequences include:

- liquidation cascades
- trading disruptions
- lending protocol instability
- loss of user confidence

---

# Risk Mitigation

Stablecoin systems attempt to reduce depeg risk through mechanisms such as:

- overcollateralization
- reserve transparency
- redemption mechanisms
- liquidity incentives

---

# Conclusion

Stablecoins are a foundational component of the DeFi ecosystem.

However, maintaining price stability under extreme market conditions remains a significant challenge.

Understanding the mechanisms that lead to depeg events is essential for evaluating stablecoin risk.
