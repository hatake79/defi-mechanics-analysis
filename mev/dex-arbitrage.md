# DEX Arbitrage

Arbitrage is one of the most fundamental forms of MEV.

It occurs when the same asset is priced differently across multiple markets.

Arbitrageurs buy the asset in the cheaper market and sell it in the more expensive one.

---

# Why Arbitrage Exists

Price differences occur because decentralized exchanges operate independently.

Each liquidity pool maintains its own pricing mechanism based on local liquidity conditions.

Example:

ETH price on DEX A = $2000  
ETH price on DEX B = $2015

An arbitrage bot can buy on DEX A and sell on DEX B.

---

# Role in Market Efficiency

Arbitrage plays an important role in maintaining price consistency.

Without arbitrageurs, decentralized exchange prices would diverge significantly from global market prices.

Arbitrage ensures that:

- prices remain aligned
- liquidity pools stay balanced

---

# Cross-Protocol Arbitrage

Advanced arbitrage strategies involve multiple protocols.

Examples include:

- DEX to CEX arbitrage
- multi-hop arbitrage across pools
- stablecoin price arbitrage

These strategies often require complex transaction routing.

---

# Automation

Arbitrage opportunities exist for only a few seconds.

Bots constantly monitor blockchain data and execute trades automatically.

Competition between arbitrage bots is extremely intense.

---

# Conclusion

DEX arbitrage is a beneficial form of MEV because it improves price discovery and market efficiency across decentralized exchanges.
