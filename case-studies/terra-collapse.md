# Terra Collapse (2022)

The collapse of the Terra ecosystem is one of the most significant failures in the history of decentralized finance.

The system relied on an algorithmic stablecoin called UST that maintained its peg through a mint-and-burn mechanism with the LUNA token.

In May 2022, the mechanism failed, resulting in tens of billions of dollars in losses.

---

# Terra Stablecoin Design

UST maintained its peg using an arbitrage mechanism.

Users could always exchange:

1 UST ↔ $1 worth of LUNA

If UST traded below $1, arbitrageurs could buy UST and redeem it for LUNA.

If UST traded above $1, users could mint UST by burning LUNA.

This mechanism relied heavily on market confidence.

---

# Anchor Protocol

A large portion of UST demand came from Anchor Protocol.

Anchor offered yields close to 20% APY on UST deposits.

This created strong demand for the stablecoin.

However, the yield was heavily subsidized and not sustainable.

---

# The Collapse

The collapse occurred in several stages.

Stage 1: Large withdrawals from Anchor reduced demand for UST.

Stage 2: UST began trading below $1.

Stage 3: Massive minting of LUNA occurred as users redeemed UST.

Stage 4: LUNA supply exploded, causing its price to collapse.

As LUNA lost value, the stabilization mechanism became ineffective.

This created a "death spiral".

---

# Consequences

The collapse resulted in:

- $40B+ market value destroyed
- major losses for investors
- severe damage to the DeFi ecosystem

Many funds and protocols had exposure to UST.

---

# Lessons

Key lessons include:

Algorithmic stablecoins without strong collateral mechanisms are highly fragile.

Sustainable yield models are critical for long-term stability.

Risk management and transparency are essential for DeFi ecosystems.
